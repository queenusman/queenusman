Biography

Queen Usman writes fiction about the things that shape people before they fully understand they are being shaped—family, desire, expectation, relationships, power, circumstance, and repetition.

Her work is interested in the space between what a person has been taught to want and what they eventually decide to want for themselves.

Her current novel, Or So She Thought, is set in 1990s Norwyn and follows Elizabeth, a young woman whose future has been carefully prepared for her. As the life she has always understood begins to become less certain, she is forced to consider a difficult question: how do you know what is yours when so much of you was formed by other people?

Her essays follow the questions her fiction leaves behind, looking at identity, relationships, autonomy, ambition, social influence, and the ordinary ways people become who they are.

Questions

The questions behind the work.

What do you write about?
I write fiction about how people become who they are—what they inherit, learn, desire, obey, repeat, and eventually question.
Why Or So She Thought?
The novel began with a question about choice: what does it mean to choose when the person doing the choosing has already been shaped by family, expectation, privilege, relationships, and circumstance?
Is the novel about escaping?
Leaving is part of Elizabeth's story, but escape is not the answer the novel is interested in. The more difficult question is what a person carries with them after they leave.
Are your essays connected to your fiction?
Yes. My fiction is where the questions become stories. My essays follow the questions those stories leave behind and examine them in ordinary life.
What does "how people become who they are" mean?
I am interested in the point where something repeated becomes familiar, where familiarity becomes expectation, and where expectation can begin to feel like part of a person rather than something they learned.
